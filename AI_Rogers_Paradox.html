Diversity vs. Efficiency

December 2025| Revisiting Rogers’ Paradox with Heterogeneous Agents on a Knowledge Map
Tech Stack:
Python NumPy SciPy Numba (JIT) Agent-Based Modeling Matplotlib

Uncovering the Trade-off Between Mastery & Innovation

In the modern era, humans increasingly learn from AI systems trained on aggregated human data. Classical "Rogers' Paradox" suggests that if social learning is too cheap, innovation stops. We extended this to the AI era: Does having a super-intelligent AI helper actually make us collectively stupider in the long run?

Previous models treated knowledge as binary (you know it or you don't). We built a novel Agent-Based Model (ABM) with a branching "Knowledge Map" to simulate cumulative innovation and the tension between "safe" local optima and "risky" global optima.

The Knowledge Map Architecture

We simulated 500 agents traversing a structured environment:

  • Low-Risk Branch: Offers fast, reliable rewards but caps at a local optimum (V ≈ 0.92).
  • High-Risk Branch: Requires a costly "fitness dip" to explore but leads to the Global Optimum (V ≈ 0.95) [Image of knowledge map architecture] .

We tested three AI strategies: No AI (Baseline), Modal AI (Copies majority), and Prestige AI (Copies the best agent).

Fig 1: The Branching Knowledge Map.

Key Findings: The Prestige Trap

Our results revealed a fundamental conflict. The Prestige AI (which mimics RLHF or "best-practices" systems) was incredibly efficient, converging to the global optimum in just ~5 generations. However, this efficiency killed discovery.

Fig 2: Prestige AI achieves high mastery (top) but suppresses discovery (middle).

Once the AI identified the "best" path, it broadcasted it to everyone. This removed the incentive for individual agents to explore, causing the High-Risk Discovery Rate to drop by ~80% compared to the baseline.

Can Diversity Save Us?

We hypothesized that introducing "risk-taking" agents (Exploration Heterogeneity) would rescue innovation.

Without AI (Blue Line): Diversity helps! We see an "inverted-U" curve where moderate diversity maximizes discovery .

With Prestige AI (Green Line): The line is flat. The AI's signal is so strong that it overrides the natural diversity of the population. The AI effectively "homogenizes" human behavior, regardless of how diverse the humans naturally are.

Summary Metrics

Metric No AI (Baseline) Prestige AI Impact
Mastery (QM) ~0.93 (Oscillates) ~0.95 (Stable) AI improves Stability
Convergence Time (Tc) ~19 Generations ~5 Generations AI is 4x Faster
Discovery Rate (RD) High (Sustained) Low (Collapsed) AI kills Innovation

Conclusion & Future Work

This project demonstrates that AI systems designed to optimize for current performance (Prestige) may inadvertently destroy the exploratory capacity needed for future breakthroughs. For Data Science, this suggests we must design RAG and Recommender systems that preserve "exploration windows" rather than creating winner-take-all feedback loops.
