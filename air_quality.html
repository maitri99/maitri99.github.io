Unpacking Implicit Sentiment

Fall 2024| Hybrid Prompting & Multi-Agent Debate in LLMs

The Challenge: Hidden Emotions

Traditional sentiment analysis struggles with "implicit sentiment"—emotional tones that are subtle, sarcastic, or indirectly expressed. For example, the sentence "and if you're not nearly moved to tears by a couple of scenes, you've got ice water in your veins" is technically a conditional statement, but it carries a "Very Positive" sentiment regarding the movie's emotional impact.

Standard methods often misclassify these nuances. To solve this, we developed a "Hybrid Prompting" framework that moves beyond simple zero-shot classification by integrating nuanced reasoning strategies.

Figure: Training and validation loss curves showing convergence for OPT-350M, OPT-125M, and SmolLM-135M models.

Methodology: Multi-Agent Debate

We tested several prompting strategies on the Stanford Sentiment Treebank (SST) dataset:

  • Role-Play Modeling: Framing the model as a film critic to encourage nuanced interpretation.
  • Chain-of-Thought (CoT): Forcing the model to generate intermediate reasoning steps.
  • Multi-Agent Debate: Simulating a debate among 5 agents (representing Very Negative to Very Positive views) with a "Moderator" making the final call.

We also performed parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) on smaller models like OPT-350M and SmolLM-135M to compare performance against larger closed-source models.

Performance comparison of prompting strategies. Hybrid prompting achieved 29% accuracy on Mistral-7B, significantly outperforming the baseline.

Key Results & Contributions

Our Hybrid Prompting strategy significantly outperformed the zero-shot baseline on the Mistral-7B model, achieving a 29% accuracy and 25% Macro F1 score.

Interestingly, for the more capable GPT-4o Mini, a simpler Role-Play strategy performed best (57.51% Accuracy), suggesting that complex hybrid prompts can sometimes over-complicate tasks for highly capable models.
